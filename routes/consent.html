Who are the researchers?

Pinzhe Zhao, a student researcher in the School of Philosophy, Psychology and Language Sciences (PPLS), will collect and analyse the data under the supervision of the Principal Investigator, Dr Bonan Zhao, School of Informatics. The named researchers and authorised University staff, where required for governance or audit, may access the data.

What is the purpose of the study?

This programme of minimal-risk research in computational cognitive science investigates how adults learn and reason about structured information, rules, and social norms. Online studies may use abstract visual patterns, symbols, sequences, rules, or grid-based scenarios to examine learning, memory, compositional reasoning, planning, coordination, and norm judgements. Later studies may refine stimuli, interfaces, parameters, analyses, or hypotheses based on earlier findings while remaining within this approved scope.

Why have I been asked to take part?

You have been invited through Prolific because you are aged 18 or over, currently live in the United Kingdom, and meet any additional eligibility criteria stated in the study listing. No specialist knowledge is required.

Do I have to take part?

No - participation in this study is entirely up to you. You can withdraw from the study at any time, without giving a reason. Your rights will not be affected. If you wish to withdraw, contact the PI. We will stop using your data in any publications or presentations submitted after you have withdrawn consent. However, we will keep copies of your original consent, and of your withdrawal request.

What will happen if I decide to take part?

Depending on the study, you may be asked to:

  • complete computer tasks involving abstract patterns, rules, or fictional grid-based scenarios. We may record choices, accuracy, response times, action sequences, your Prolific Participant ID, and limited demographic information. We will not ask for your name or personal email address, and IP addresses will not be retained in the research dataset.
  • take part in an online Prolific task. You may classify or remember information, infer rules, plan or coordinate actions, or judge compliance or violations in fictional scenarios. Any computer agent will be clearly identified, and no undisclosed deception will be used.
  • complete one study, usually lasting approximately 30 minutes; its expected duration will be stated in the Prolific listing before consent.
  • take part without any audio or video recording.
  • complete one online session at a time and place of your choice using a computer.

Compensation

The base payment and expected duration will be shown in the Prolific listing. Payment will be made through Prolific.

Each task allows up to Runs. Solving it on your Run still counts as success. If some robots are still not home after Run , you move on with your last selections.

You will earn a £0.10 bonus for each task you solve successfully. Your base payment will not be affected.

Are there any risks associated with taking part?

There are no significant risks associated with participation. Possible discomfort is limited to ordinary screen fatigue, mild frustration, or judging fictional norm violations; you may stop if you feel uncomfortable.

Are there any benefits associated with taking part?

There is no guaranteed personal benefit beyond the compensation stated in the Prolific listing. The research may improve understanding of how people learn, reason, make decisions, and respond to rules and social norms.

What will happen to the results of this study?

The results of this study may be summarised in published articles, reports and presentations. Quotes or key findings will be anonymized: We will remove any information that could, in our assessment, allow anyone to identify you. With your consent, information can also be used for future research. Your data may be archived for a minimum of two years. Consent records and any Prolific-ID linkage will be stored separately from research responses. Anonymised data may be retained for future ethically approved research.

Data protection and confidentiality

Your data will be processed in accordance with Data Protection Law. All information collected about you will be kept strictly confidential. Your data will be referred to by a unique participant number rather than by name; your Prolific Participant ID will be replaced with a study code and the linking file stored separately. Your data will only be viewed by the researcher/research team: Pinzhe Zhao, Dr Bonan Zhao, and authorised University governance or audit staff.

All electronic data will be stored on a password-protected encrypted computer, on the School of Informatics' secure file servers, or on the University's secure encrypted cloud storage services (DataShare, ownCloud, or Sharepoint) and all paper records will be stored in a locked filing cabinet in the PI's office. Your consent information will be kept separately from your responses in order to minimise risk.

What are my data protection rights?

The University of Edinburgh is a Data Controller for the information you provide. You have the right to access information held about you. Your right of access can be exercised in accordance Data Protection Law. You also have other rights including rights of correction, erasure and objection. After the ID link is removed or findings are aggregated, your individual data may no longer be identifiable or removable.

For more details, including the right to lodge a complaint with the Information Commissioner's Office, please visit www.ico.org.uk. Questions, comments and requests about your personal data can also be sent to the University Data Protection Officer at dpo@ed.ac.uk. For general information about how we use your data, visit the University research privacy notice.

Who can I contact?

If you wish to make a complaint about the study, please contact inf-ethics@inf.ed.ac.uk. When you contact us, please provide the study title and detail the nature of your complaint.
